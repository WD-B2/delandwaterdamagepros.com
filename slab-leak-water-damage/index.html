Slab leak water damage is the harm done by a leaking pipe under or inside a concrete slab foundation. Most Deland homes are built slab-on-grade, which means supply lines run through or under concrete, and a pinhole leak can run for weeks. Water then rises through cracks or travels to the nearest soft spot and soaks flooring and baseboards.

Warm spots, wet floors and a high water bill: could it be a slab leak?

Several signs point to a slab leak. Hot spots on a floor suggest a leaking hot water line, and a damp patch under carpet or a cracked tile suggests a cold line. A water bill that rises with no change in use is another clue, and the sound of running water in a quiet house is a third.

Check your meter with every fixture off. If the leak indicator dial turns, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the house. A plumber uses acoustic detection and pressure testing to find the exact spot, and that is the first step before any floor is opened.

Why slab leaks happen in Volusia County homes

Copper lines in older Florida houses can pit from the outside where they touch soil or concrete, and aggressive water chemistry can eat from the inside. Newer homes may use PEX, which fails differently. Knowing the pipe type helps a plumber decide whether to repair or re-route lines.

The City of Deland Utilities department supplies water and can confirm if a high bill is tied to a main on the street side. Ask them whether the meter reading and usage history show the leak is on your side of the meter.

How crews dry a slab leak loss

Crews extract surface water and use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map wet flooring and walls. Floating flooring and carpet are lifted so concrete can dry. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings reach drying goals.

Concrete holds water longer than drywall, so drying takes patience and monitoring. The structural drying page explains why the equipment stays several days.

What affects slab leak restoration price

  1. How long water ran under the slab.
  2. Flooring type and how much must be lifted.
  3. Whether walls took moisture.
  4. Drying equipment days, which are longer for concrete.
  5. Whether mold is found.

Insurance and slab leaks

Coverage for slab leaks varies widely by policy. Some pay for resulting water damage and a limited cost of access, while others exclude them. Read your policy and report within one year under Florida Statute 627.70132.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Yes. The line is answered 24/7. Shut the main valve and call. A crew is arranged to extract water and begin drying once the address is confirmed.

How do I know I have a slab leak?

Warm floor spots, a meter that moves with fixtures off, a sudden high water bill and the sound of running water are common signs. A plumber confirms with detection equipment.

Why is my floor warm in one spot?

A leaking hot line under the slab can heat the concrete above it. Call a plumber to confirm.

Does a slab leak damage the foundation?

Long leaks can wash soil from under the slab, so ask a plumber or engineer if you see cracks.
