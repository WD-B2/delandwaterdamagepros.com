What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in Deland, FL

Porous items that soaked in contaminated water usually go. Hard, non-porous items can usually be cleaned. Here is how to sort by material.

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

After a water loss, you face dozens of small decisions about what to save. The rule of thumb is that the cleaner the water and the harder the surface, the better the odds. This guide sorts common household items by material and by water type so you can decide quickly.

Before you throw anything away, photograph it and list it, because an adjuster may ask for an inventory. If the item is bulky or hazardous, keep a photo and a note instead.

Start with the water type

Clean water from a supply line is the best case. Gray water, such as dishwasher or washing machine discharge, carries some contamination. Sewage and flood water are the worst, and porous items that soaked in them are usually discarded.

ItemClean waterSewage or flood water
Hard plastic, glass, metal, glazed ceramicClean and dryClean and disinfect, or discard if damaged
Solid wood furnitureDry slowly, may be savedOften discard or restore professionally
Upholstered furniture and mattressesOften saved if dried fastDiscard
Carpet and padCarpet may be saved, pad usually replacedDiscard both
Drywall and insulationDry in place or cut out if soakedCut out and discard
Cardboard, books, paperDry quickly or discard if softDiscard unless irreplaceable

Items that usually can be saved

  • Glass, metal and hard plastic items, washed and dried.
  • Solid wood furniture that was clean-water wet and dried slowly.
  • Clothing and linens from clean water, washed promptly.
  • Dishes and cookware after cleaning and sanitizing.
  • Photos and documents, if handled correctly (see our separate guide).

Wash fabrics soon, and dry them fully before putting them away. Items that were wet for days and smell musty are best discarded.

Items that usually go

  • Anything porous that soaked in sewage or flood water.
  • Carpet pad in nearly every case.
  • Mattresses and upholstered pieces that stayed wet.
  • Particleboard furniture that has swollen.
  • Food and medicine that touched floodwater.
  • Stuffed toys and pillows from contaminated water.

If you are unsure, take a photo and ask. Our crews can tell you if drywall or flooring is salvageable after a moisture check. The carpet page and hardwood page explain two common cases.

How to sort efficiently

1

Photograph

Take pictures of each item and the room.

2

Sort

Make piles for keep, clean, ask and discard.

3

Move

Take salvageable items to a dry, ventilated area.

4

Record

List discarded items and keep samples if the adjuster wants them.

Local tips for Deland households

After Hurricane Ian in September 2022, local reporting recorded roughly 13 to 15 inches of rain in Deland, and many residents learned that a waiting adjuster means items sit wet longer. Take dated photos and list belongings quickly, then move wet items out of the house so they stop feeding humidity.

Pickup schedules can change after a major storm, so check with your waste provider before you put large items at the curb.

Room by room: where the decisions get hard

Kitchens are the hardest, since food, pots and appliances are all in play. Discard open food that touched water and anything in a cardboard box. Cookware and dishes can be washed in hot water and sanitized, while a dishwasher or fridge that sat in water needs a technician before you use it again.

Bedrooms usually mean mattresses, clothing and books. Closet floors often hold shoes and boxes that wicked water up from the slab. Pull everything off the floor and check the lowest shelf, since that is where the hidden damage collects.

Garages and storage rooms hold paint, chemicals and tools. Sort them carefully, since wet cardboard hides spills and rust. Set aside anything flammable and ask your waste provider about safe disposal.

Safety when handling wet belongings

Wear gloves and boots when handling items from contaminated water, and wash your hands afterward. Do not bring wet cardboard or fabric into clean areas of the house. Bag discarded items and keep them out of living spaces.

Electronics that were wet should not be plugged in until a technician has checked them. Water in appliances can cause shocks or fires.

Keep a running inventory for the claim

A simple spreadsheet or a notes app works. List the item, its age, where you bought it and the estimated cost to replace, then attach the photo. Do this as you sort, not afterward, because memory fades fast during cleanup.

Keep receipts or card statements for big items when you can find them. They support the value you list, and they speed up a contents claim.

If the loss is large

Call a crew before you start hauling, since removal of some materials is part of the mitigation plan. Neighbors in Glenwood and Cassadaga should mention if they have septic or well systems, because contaminated water can affect those too. For help sorting a sewage loss, see our sewage backup page.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can I keep a mattress that got wet?

If it was clean water and dried fast, sometimes. A mattress that soaked in contaminated water is discarded.

Should I throw out food that touched floodwater?

Yes. Discard food and medicine that touched floodwater.

Can wet books be saved?

Some can, if handled quickly. Paper and books are covered in our guide on photos and documents.

What do I do with items the adjuster wants to see?

Photograph them and keep them in a bagged area until the adjuster has seen them, if it is safe.
