Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Deland, FL

Kitchens combine plumbing, appliances and cabinets in one tight space. Crews find hidden moisture and dry cabinets and floors before they swell or rot.

Kitchen water damage restoration is the drying and repair of cabinets, flooring, walls and subfloor after water from a sink, dishwasher, fridge line or supply pipe. The kitchen has more water sources than any other room, and most leaks start behind cabinets where you cannot see them. If a cabinet base feels soft or the floor near the sink has started to lift, call and describe it.

Shut off the water supply under the sink if you can, and empty the cabinet so the crew can reach the walls and floor. Photograph the area and keep the failed part.

Where kitchen water damage starts

Sink supply lines and drains are the leading cause, followed by dishwashers, ice maker lines and the supply to a pot filler. A drip that falls on a cabinet floor can run for months and rot the base from the inside. Run your hand along the underside of the sink cabinet twice a year, and replace braided lines at the first sign of corrosion.

Slab-on-grade kitchens hide water differently, because the floor beneath cabinets is often continuous with the main floor. Water moves out under cabinet toe kicks and into adjacent rooms, so the damage can be larger than the wet area suggests. The appliance leak page covers dishwashers and ice makers in more detail.

Signs of kitchen water damage

Soft or swollen cabinet base

Particleboard swells when wet and does not recover.

Floor lifting near the sink

Laminate and engineered wood cup first.

Musty smell under the sink

Damp cabinet interiors hold odor.

Stains on the ceiling below

A second-floor kitchen can drip onto rooms beneath.

What to do in the first hours

Shut off the supply valve under the sink or behind the appliance, then take everything out of the wet cabinet. Dry the cabinet floor with towels, and prop doors open for airflow. Do not run the dishwasher or washer until a plumber confirms the leak is fixed.

Photograph the cabinet interior, the floor and the failed part before anything is thrown away. Keep receipts for recent plumbing repairs, since they show the maintenance history to an adjuster.

Rebuilding the kitchen after drying

Repairs begin once readings are normal. Toe kicks and base cabinets are replaced, flooring is patched or replaced and walls are patched and painted. Matching a discontinued cabinet door or a floor pattern can take time, so order materials early.

If the kitchen sits on a slab, the new flooring should wait until the concrete and subfloor are dry. Installing over damp materials is a common cause of cupped floors that return.

How crews dry a kitchen

Crews remove the toe kicks and drawers, check moisture with meters and thermal imaging, and open the cabinet back if needed. Extraction units remove surface water, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the area. Countertops usually stay in place, and cabinets are removed only when they cannot be dried.

Plumbers fix the leak and appliance techs repair appliances. We plan the drying around them, so the kitchen is back in use as soon as readings allow.

What affects kitchen restoration price

  1. Size of the wet area and the room below.
  2. Cabinet and flooring materials.
  3. Whether countertops must come off.
  4. Drying equipment days.
  5. Whether mold has started in cabinet bases.

Insurance and kitchen leaks

Sudden failures such as a split supply line are commonly covered for resulting damage, while slow drips over months can be excluded. Report within one year under Florida Statute 627.70132 and keep the failed part.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can wet kitchen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood and plywood often can be dried if caught early. Particleboard rarely recovers once soaked.

How do I know water is under my kitchen floor?

Cupping, soft spots, a musty smell and stained cabinet bases are common signs. Moisture meters confirm it.

Do I need to remove my countertops?

Not usually. Countertops stay unless the cabinets beneath them must be removed.

Who fixes the leak?

A plumber or appliance technician fixes the source. We dry and restore the damage.
