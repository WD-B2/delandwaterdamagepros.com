How the age of your Orange City home changes the damage

Homes from the 1980s and 1990s often have supply lines and water heaters that have reached the end of their service life. Replace braided hoses and check the heater for rust at the base, because failures at that age come suddenly. A line that bursts in an attic or a wall can bring down a ceiling.

Newer subdivisions use PEX and CPVC lines that fail at fittings and bends. Leaks there are small and slow, and they show up as a stain on the ceiling below or a soft spot behind a vanity. A moisture meter finds those faster than a flashlight does.

Older downtown buildings near Volusia Avenue have flat or low-slope roofs and old flashing. The 1929 city hall at 205 East Graves Avenue is a good example, since it had water intrusion and later a roof restoration. If your building is that old, check roof drains and flashing before each rainy season.

If water is still spreading in Orange City

Steps while you wait

  • Close the main shutoff near the meter, or call Orange City Utilities if the valve will not turn.
  • Cut power to wet areas only from a dry breaker and stay out of rooms with water at outlets.
  • Move rugs, shoes and small furniture to dry ground and take photos of the water line.
  • Keep everyone out of water that came from a toilet, tub or floor drain.
