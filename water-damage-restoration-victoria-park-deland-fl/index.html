Victoria Park is a large planned community in the southeast part of DeLand. How Victoria Park is organized

Real estate listings group homes into sections such as Victoria Hills, Victoria Trails, Victoria Commons and Cresswind at Victoria Gardens, an age-restricted community inside the same master plan. Those names are subdivision and marketing labels, not city districts, so give the line your street name along with your section. Listings describe homes built from the early 2000s onward, and most addresses use ZIP 32724.

Cresswind at Victoria Gardens is separate from the newer Cresswind community on the east side of town, which has its own page for Lake Winnemissett. If your community has a homeowners association, check whether it needs notice before outside work begins.

Where water usually comes from in a single-family home

We do not have local claim statistics for Victoria Park, so this page sticks to how water damage is handled in any home. Start by finding the source, because drying cannot begin until the water has stopped. The table below lists the usual sources and the first safe step for each.

Before you touch anything

Keep people and pets out of any room where water may reach outlets, cords or appliances.

Do not stand in water to reach a breaker. If the panel is wet or the floor is flooded, call your electric utility or an electrician.

Treat water from a sewer line or from outdoors as contaminated, and do not clean it up with a household vacuum or fans.

What extraction and drying involve

A restoration crew removes standing water first, then measures moisture in drywall, trim and flooring with a meter. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to dry materials in place when that is possible. Readings are repeated over several visits until wet materials return to a normal range, and materials that cannot dry are removed. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the published reference for how losses are assessed and documented, and you can ask any company how it applies that standard.

Fans alone rarely dry a wall cavity or the layer under flooring. That is why water extraction and structural drying are usually done together. Ask for a copy of the moisture readings before the job ends.

Storms, rain and flood maps

Hurricane Ian in September 2022 brought about 13 inches of rain to DeLand, according to a county briefing reported by the Daytona Beach News-Journal, and the National Weather Service issued flood warnings for the St. Johns River near DeLand. That was a citywide and regional event, and we have no record of how it affected Victoria Park streets in particular.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for this page. Different parts of a community can fall in different map zones, so search your own address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. Our flood damage page explains cleanup after outdoor water enters a home.

Records that help with an insurance claim

Photograph the damage and the source before anything is moved. Keep receipts, a list of damaged belongings and the moisture readings from the drying visits. Whether your policy covers a loss depends on its wording and on the cause, so call your insurer or agent early and ask what they need.

Questions we hear in Victoria Park

Does this page cover Cresswind at Victoria Gardens?

Yes. Cresswind at Victoria Gardens sits inside the Victoria Park master plan, so the same first steps apply. Tell the line the name of your community so any association contact is considered.

What ZIP code do Victoria Park addresses use?

Listings show most addresses in ZIP 32724. ZIP codes follow the post office and not neighborhood lines, so give the line your full street address either way.

Should I wait for my insurance adjuster before drying starts?

Photograph everything first. Many policies expect you to take reasonable steps to stop further damage, but the exact duties are in your policy, so confirm with your insurer.

Can a homeowners association stop emergency drying?

Associations usually have rules about exterior work, not about stopping water inside your home. Read your community documents and notify the association if the work affects shared areas.
