AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Deland, FL

Air conditioners run nearly year-round in Central Florida, and a clogged drain line can drip into walls and ceilings for weeks.

AC leak water damage is the moisture harm caused when a condensate line clogs, a drain pan overflows or a coil freezes and thaws, and the water ends up in ceilings, walls or the floor. In Deland, systems run most of the year, and an attic air handler can drip onto insulation for weeks before a stain appears. If you see a stain or a wet closet, call and describe it, and shut the system off to stop more water.

Turning the AC off at the thermostat stops condensate production. A clogged drain line is the most common cause, and a technician can clear it. Our job is to dry the wet materials and repair what the water damaged.

Why Central Florida AC systems leak

High humidity means an air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air every day, and all of it must drain away. Algae and slime build up in the condensate line and block it. Pour a cup of vinegar into the access port every few months, or schedule a line flush with your HVAC service so the line stays clear.

A secondary drain pan under an attic unit has an overflow switch in many homes. If that switch fails or is missing, water drops through the ceiling below. Test the float switch at your next tune-up so the system shuts off before water reaches the drywall.

Closets with air handlers share walls with bedrooms and hallways. Water there wicks into baseboards and carpet and can feed mold in the cavity.

Signs of an AC leak

Ceiling stain near a vent or attic hatch

Rings that dry and reappear point to condensate.

Water in the drain pan or closet floor

A full pan means the line is blocked.

Musty smell when the system runs

Damp insulation and ductwork hold odor.

Ice on the coil or lines

Frozen coils thaw and overflow the pan.

What to do while the system is off

Place towels or a wet vacuum under any active drip, and move belongings out of closets that share a wall with the air handler. Check the attic hatch or closet for a full drain pan, and photograph it before anyone empties it. Those photos show an adjuster how the leak started.

Wet insulation can look dry on top while the bottom layer stays soaked against the ceiling drywall. Do not walk on ceiling joists in the attic to check, because a missed step puts a foot through the ceiling. Call and let a crew read the moisture from below with a meter.

Keeping the next AC leak from happening

Schedule a drain line flush when the system is serviced, and ask the technician to confirm the float switch shuts the unit off when the pan fills. Replace a cracked or sagging line that holds water in a low spot. A small repair at a tune-up costs far less than reopening a ceiling.

How crews dry and repair AC water damage

Crews check moisture in ceilings, walls and attic insulation with meters and thermal imaging. Wet insulation is removed, and air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the cavity. Drywall that stayed wet is cut out and replaced.

If mold is present, the mold remediation page explains the next step. An HVAC technician should fix the drain and pan before we rebuild.

Insurance and AC leaks

Policies often treat sudden overflow differently from a slow leak that went unnoticed for weeks, and wording varies. Report early, and keep records of AC service visits. Florida Statute 627.70132 sets the one year notice window.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can I keep running the AC if it is leaking?

Shut it off until the drain is cleared. Running it keeps producing water that spills into the house.

How do I clear a clogged condensate line?

Many homeowners pour vinegar into the access port, though an HVAC technician can flush it properly. Call one if the line stays blocked.

Can an AC leak cause mold?

Yes. Damp insulation and drywall can grow mold within days in warm weather.

Who fixes the leak, you or an HVAC company?

An HVAC technician fixes the equipment. We dry and repair the water damage.

AC dripping into the house?

Turn the system off and call. We will explain the next steps.
