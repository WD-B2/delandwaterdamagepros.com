Saving Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Deland, FL

Paper and photos survive best when you stop the damage early. Here is what to do in the first day.

24/7 line
(386) 699-0141
Call now

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

Family photos, legal papers and keepsakes are hard to replace, and they are also fragile when wet. The first day matters most, because wet paper starts to grow mold and photos stick together as they dry. These steps follow widely used guidance for handling water-damaged paper, and a professional conservator can help with items of real value.

First, protect yourself and the items

Wear gloves, since flood water and sewage carry contamination. Move items from the wet area to a clean, dry place with good airflow. Handle them gently and support wet paper from below, because it tears easily.

If the water was contaminated, treat the items with care and consider whether they are worth saving. Irreplaceable photos can be rinsed gently with clean water, while ordinary paper is better scanned or replaced.

Photos

1

Separate

Gently separate photos from each other and from frames or albums while they are still wet.

2

Rinse

If muddy, rinse gently in clean water. Do not rub the surface.

3

Dry

Lay them face up on a clean, absorbent surface such as paper towels or a clean cloth.

4

Replace

Change the surface as it becomes wet and keep photos out of direct sun.

If you cannot deal with them within a day or two, put them in a freezer bag and freeze them. Freezing stops damage and gives you time to dry them later.

Avoid hair dryers, ovens or direct sun, which can warp paper and harden the damage. Avoid sealing wet items in plastic bags unless you are freezing them. Wet paper in a closed bag grows mold quickly.

Make copies and record details

Photograph or scan important documents while they are still legible. Make a list of what you saved and what was lost, since you may need it for an insurance contents claim and for replacement requests. Keep these records away from the wet area.

Deland-specific resources and risks

Deeds are recorded with the county, and permits come from the building office that issued them. Call the relevant office before you assume a document is lost for good.

Deland humidity slows drying and speeds mold on paper. Run your AC, use a dehumidifier in the room where items dry and keep the room closed so the humid outdoor air does not undo your work.

Other keepsakes: fabric, leather and wood

Quilts, wedding dresses and family linens can often be washed if the water was clean, but do it soon and dry them flat in shade. Leather should dry slowly away from heat, since heat makes it stiff and cracked. Wooden keepsakes dry best in a cool room with air moving around them, and quick drying in the sun can split the grain.

If an item holds both sentimental and monetary value, photograph it before you touch it and write down what you did. Those notes help with a contents claim, and they help a conservator choose a treatment.

Set up a drying area

Choose a room with a working AC and a dehumidifier, and cover tables with clean paper towels or blotting paper. Space items out so air reaches every side. Check the area morning and night, and change blotting paper as it soaks up water.

What to do about the structure

Saving belongings means little if the house stays wet. Mold in a closet or an archive box can spread to papers within days, so dry the structure while you dry the items. Our structural drying page explains how crews check moisture in walls and floors.

Water in a basement-like lower level or a crawl space often soaks stored boxes first. See our lower-level page and water extraction page for how that cleanup works.

A note on digital copies

Once items are dry, scan or photograph them at a good resolution and store copies in two places, such as a cloud account and a flash drive kept off site. Digital copies do not replace originals, but they protect you from the next loss. Label files by date and subject so you can find them quickly.

When to call a conservator

  • Rare books, old maps and original artwork.
  • Photos with historic or family value that are sticking together.
  • Documents with ink that is running.
  • Anything that has already grown mold.

A conservator can stabilize the items and advise whether treatment is worth the cost. Freeze valuable items while you search for one.

Residents of Orange City and Lake Helen should keep a copy of critical documents in a safe place off site, which also protects them in hurricane season.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can wet photos be saved?

Often yes if you separate them and dry them quickly, or freeze them to stop damage until you can.

Should I freeze wet documents?

Freezing is a standard way to stop damage when you cannot dry items right away. Use a freezer bag.

Can moldy papers be saved?

Some can, but moldy items need care. Valuable pieces are best handled by a conservator.

Where do I replace deeds or permits in Deland?

Contact the issuing office, such as the county for recorded deeds or the building office that issued a permit, to ask about copies.
