Mold Removal Companies in Deland, FL: How to Compare

Three kinds of companies show up in this search, and they do different jobs. Know the difference before you collect quotes.

24/7 line
(386) 699-0141
Call now

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

Search results for mold removal companies mix several kinds of businesses together, and each does a different job. A homeowner who knows which one is which can ask better questions and avoid paying twice. This guide explains the roles and a simple way to compare quotes.

The three roles in a mold job

RoleWhat they doWatch for
Mold assessorInspects, samples and writes a protocol for the workShould not also do the removal on the same job
Mold remediatorContains, removes and dries the affected areaNeeds a written scope and a plan for the moisture source
General contractor or restoration companyFixes the leak, dries and rebuildsMay bring in a licensed remediator for the mold step

Florida licenses mold assessors and remediators separately under Chapter 468, Part XVI, through the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. The separation exists so the party who tests has no stake in selling the removal. Ask each company which role it plays.

Which one do you need first?

If you can see growth and know the source, a remediator can scope the job. If you smell mold and see nothing, or if a buyer or lender wants documentation, an assessor comes first. If water is still active, stop the water source before either one starts.

Our mold remediation page explains the removal process, and our structural drying page explains the moisture control that keeps growth from returning.

How to compare three quotes

  • Same affected area and same scope of materials in each quote.
  • Containment and air filtration listed in each.
  • Moisture source addressed or excluded, in writing.
  • Testing and clearance described, with who does it.
  • Rebuild included or excluded, so totals compare fairly.
  • Timeline and what could change the price.

A quote that is far below the others often leaves something out. Ask what is missing before you accept the savings.

What a site visit should include

A real estimate starts with a visit. The estimator should check moisture readings, look in the attic or crawl space if the leak came from there and open a small inspection hole if growth is suspected behind a wall. A quote given over the phone cannot account for any of that.

Ask the estimator to explain what they found in plain words and to mark the affected area on a sketch. That sketch becomes the shared reference for scope, containment and clearance later.

Timeline you can expect

Small jobs can finish in a few days, while larger ones with containment, drying and a clearance test take longer. Drying is the part that surprises people, because the structure has to reach normal moisture readings before it is closed up. Plan for the rebuild separately, since drywall and paint add their own days.

If a company promises a one day job for a large area, ask how the drying and clearance fit into that day. Rushed work is the usual reason for a second round.

What drives price in a mold job

  1. Size and location of the affected area.
  2. Whether porous materials such as drywall and insulation must come out.
  3. Containment and air filtration requirements.
  4. Testing before and after removal.
  5. Drying time and rebuild work.

Mold in an attic or crawl space costs differently from mold in a bathroom, since access and containment differ. See our crawl space page for under-house work.

Deland context for your comparison

Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591 can tell you whether repairs after removal need a permit. Ask before demolition so your schedule includes the permit time.

City of Deland Utilities handles water and wastewater inside the city, and an unexplained rise in your water bill is a reason to check for a hidden leak that feeds mold. Compare your last few bills before you call anyone.

Insurance and mold removal

Mold coverage varies a great deal, and many policies limit or exclude it unless it follows a covered water loss. Ask your agent before you hire. Florida Statute 627.70132 sets a one year notice window for property claims.

Homeowners in Orange City and De Leon Springs should confirm that a company serves their address before comparing prices.

Paperwork you should receive

At the end of a job you should have the written scope, a drying or work log, photos, the clearance report if one was done and the invoice. Store these with your insurance and sale documents. A buyer or an adjuster may ask for them years later.

Questions about the rebuild

Most mold quotes cover removal and drying but not the rebuild. Ask who will replace the drywall, insulation and trim, and whether that is priced in the same quote or separately. If you use a different contractor for the rebuild, share the clearance report so they know the area is ready.

Questions we hear in Deland

What is the difference between a mold assessor and a remediator?

An assessor inspects and tests, and a remediator removes the mold. Florida licenses them separately.

Should I hire the same company to test and remove mold?

Independent testing gives results more weight, so many people use a separate party for clearance.

How many quotes should I get for mold removal?

Two or three on the same scope is a good start.

Where do I check a mold license in Florida?

The Department of Business and Professional Regulation lists licensed mold assessors and remediators.
