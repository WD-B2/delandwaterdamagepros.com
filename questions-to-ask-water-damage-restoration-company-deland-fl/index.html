Questions to Ask a Restoration Company in Deland, FL

Good answers to a few plain questions tell you more than a polished website. Use this list on the phone before anyone comes out.

24/7 line
(386) 699-0141
Call now

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

When water is on the floor, people hire the first company that answers. That is understandable, but a few questions asked on that first call separate crews that document and dry properly from crews that pump and leave. The questions below are short enough to ask while you are mopping.

Ask them calmly and write down the answers. A good company is glad to explain, and the way they answer is itself information.

Questions about the first visit

  • What will you do when you arrive, and in what order?
  • Will you check moisture with meters and thermal imaging before you start?
  • Will you explain the scope and the price drivers before work starts?
  • Who will be on site, and who is my contact?
  • What do you need from me, such as access, shutoff locations or a key?

Beware of any company that can give a firm price by phone without seeing the water. Scope depends on what is behind walls and under floors, and an honest company says so.

Questions about equipment and drying

  • Which equipment will you use, and how many pieces?
  • How will you measure that the structure is dry?
  • How often will you come back to check readings?
  • What are the drying goals for each material?
  • Will I get a copy of the drying log?

The drying log matters because it shows what was wet, when it was checked and when it reached normal readings. Without one, you are trusting a handshake. Our structural drying page explains how crews set drying goals.

Questions about price and paperwork

No company can tell you what your insurer will pay, because the policy decides. Ask what records you will get to support a claim.

Questions about experience and credentials

  • How long has the company worked in Volusia County?
  • Do you hold the licenses and insurance required for this work?
  • Can you describe a recent job like mine?
  • Do you subcontract any of this work, and to whom?
  • What happens if hidden damage turns up?

Be careful with claims that cannot be checked. Ask for specifics, and verify licenses that the state publishes. For mold work in Florida, licenses are issued by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation.

Local checks for Deland

Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591 can tell you if your repairs need a permit. A reliable company asks about permits before they start demolition.

If the water might be coming from the city side, such as a main break or a sewer backup, City of Deland Utilities at 386-626-7051 handles that, and a restoration company can only clean up what comes into your home.

Questions that depend on the type of loss

For sewage, ask how the area will be contained and how contaminated materials will be handled. For a ceiling leak, ask whether they will coordinate with a roofer or plumber. For a commercial space, ask how work will be phased so the business can keep running.

See our sewage backup page and commercial page for what good answers look like in those cases. Homeowners in Glenwood and Orange City should also ask whether the company serves their address and how far crews travel.

A short script for the first call

You can use this wording: I have water in my house from a leak, I have shut off the main valve and I need to know what you will do first and what it costs to start. Then listen. A good coordinator asks about the source, the depth, the rooms and the age of the house before they answer.

Ask how soon a crew can arrive and whether that depends on conditions. Treat any promise of an exact arrival time as a reason to ask more questions, since traffic, weather and other emergencies change schedules.

After the first call

Ask the company to send the scope and terms in writing, and compare them with a second company if time allows. If water is still spreading, do not wait for a second opinion before dealing with the source. Shut the main valve, then call.

Keep every document, receipt and photo in one folder. If you later file or supplement an insurance claim, those records are what you will use.

Questions we hear in Deland

What is the most important question to ask a restoration company?

How will you measure that my house is dry, and will I get the log? That one answer shows whether they document the work.

Should a restoration company give a price over the phone?

A rough explanation of price drivers is fine. A firm price without seeing the water is a warning sign.

Do I need to verify licensing for water damage work in Florida?

Ask about licenses and insurance for the work being done. For mold, the state licenses assessors and remediators.

Who do I call if the water comes from the city main?

Call City of Deland Utilities at 386-626-7051 for the main, and a restoration company for the inside cleanup.
