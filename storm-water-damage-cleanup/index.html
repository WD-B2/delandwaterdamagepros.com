Storm water damage is an emergency, so call the 24/7 line once it is safe to do so and describe where the water is entering. Stay away from downed lines and standing water, shut off power at a dry breaker if you can and photograph the damage. Roof tarping is a roofer's job, and we dry the interior while that is arranged.

Storm water damage cleanup covers the water that enters a building through roof leaks, broken windows, flooded doorways and wind-driven rain. Central Florida storms range from afternoon thunderstorms to hurricanes, and each pushes water in differently. The common thread is water arriving fast and in places the home is not designed to drain.

Storm damage that cannot wait

Call as soon as the weather clears if water is dripping, a window is broken or a ceiling is sagging. Same-day response is offered when available after smaller storms, but after hurricanes demand rises and roads may be blocked. Describe your situation clearly so crews can prioritize active water.

Storm-day hazards

Stay away from downed power lines and call the power utility or 911. Do not enter water that may be energized.

Watch for roof debris, broken glass and weakened ceilings. Avoid walking under sagging drywall.

Storms that have hit Deland and Volusia County

Hurricane Ian in September 2022 brought roughly 13 to 15 inches of rain to Deland, according to local reporting of National Weather Service figures. The St. Johns River near Deland reached a record 6.2 feet. Homes with older roofs and window seals took the most interior water, so inspect both before each hurricane season starts on June 1.

Hurricane Milton in October 2024 caused flooding in Volusia County, and the county fairgrounds in Deland opened as a shelter. After a storm, document damage with dated photos and a video walk-through before cleanup, since adjusters often have large backlogs.

Summer thunderstorms are more frequent and cause their own damage, such as wind-driven rain through gable vents and clogged gutters. A small repair before storm season prevents a large interior loss.

How crews clean up after a storm

1

Stabilize

Standing water is pumped and loose debris cleared.

2

Find

Moisture meters and thermal imaging locate wet ceilings, walls and insulation.

3

Remove

Saturated drywall and insulation are cut out where drying in place would fail.

4

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure.

A roofer tarps and repairs the roof. We coordinate timing so the drying is not undone by a second leak. Our ceiling repair page covers the interior rebuild, and the flood damage page covers river and surge water.

What affects storm cleanup price

  1. Number of rooms and floors affected.
  2. Whether roof or window openings need temporary protection.
  3. Materials that must be removed.
  4. Drying equipment days.
  5. Demand after a major storm, which can affect scheduling.

Insurance after a storm

Windstorm and hurricane coverage often has a separate deductible, and flood coverage is separate again. Read your policy and call your agent before filing. Florida Statute 627.70132 requires notice of a property claim within one year of the date of loss.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.
