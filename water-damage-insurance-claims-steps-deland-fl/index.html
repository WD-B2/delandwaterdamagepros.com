Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps in Deland, FL

A claim goes better when the first hours are documented. Follow these steps from the first photo to the final repair record.

24/7 line
(386) 699-0141
Call now

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

A water damage claim is easier when you can show what happened, when it happened and what you did about it. This guide walks through the order of steps, from the first hour to the last repair invoice. It is general information, so check your own policy for its specific rules.

Step 1: Make the situation safe and stop the water

Shut off the source if you can reach it, usually the main water valve. Turn off power to wet areas only from a dry breaker, and leave sewage or flood water to a crew. Photograph the source before anyone repairs it, because the failed part is evidence.

Step 2: Document before anything moves

Take wide photos of each affected room, then close-ups of damage, such as stains, swollen baseboards and the water line on walls. A short video walk-through adds context. Note the date and time, and write down what you saw and when.

Keep the failed hose, valve or fitting if a plumber removes it. An adjuster may ask to see it, and it supports the claim that the failure was sudden.

Step 3: Notify your carrier

Call the number on your policy or app and report the loss. Florida Statute 627.70132 requires notice of an initial or reopened claim within one year of the date of loss, but earlier is better because carriers may ask about steps you took right away. Write down the claim number, the adjuster name and the date of every call.

Step 4: Start mitigation

Policies commonly expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. That means water removal and drying should begin without waiting for the adjuster to visit, and receipts for that work are part of the claim. Step 5: Meet the adjuster

Walk the adjuster through the affected areas and show your photos. Ask what the policy covers and what documents they need next. If you disagree with a scope or amount, ask for the reasoning in writing and bring your crew's records.

Local notes for Deland claims

Volusia County Building and Code Administration, at 386-626-6591, can tell you whether repairs need a permit. Ask early, since a permit requirement can change your schedule and your estimate.

After major storms such as Hurricane Ian in September 2022, local reporting recorded roughly 13 to 15 inches of rain in Deland. In high-volume periods, adjusters take longer to arrive, so thorough photos and readings help you move on without waiting.

Step 6: Get written estimates

Ask the crew for a written scope that lists rooms, materials and equipment. Compare it with the adjuster's estimate, and ask the crew to explain any differences. A clear scope helps avoid disputes later.

Step 7: Repair and keep records

Repairs follow drying. Keep invoices for every stage, and keep before and after photos. If your claim has a supplement, such as hidden damage found during demolition, report it promptly to your adjuster.

How long the process takes

Timelines vary with the size of the loss and the carrier. Mitigation takes days, an adjuster visit may take days or weeks and repairs are scheduled once the estimate is agreed. Keep notes of each step, because dates matter if a dispute comes up.

If the claim stalls, call your adjuster and ask what is outstanding. A polite written follow-up with your photos and drying logs attached often moves things along.

Working with contractors during a claim

Get written scopes and keep copies of every estimate. If the adjuster's estimate is lower than a contractor's, ask both to explain line by line where they differ. Often the gap is a line item such as insulation, baseboards or drying days, which can be reviewed.

Common mistakes that slow a claim

The most common mistakes are throwing away damaged items before they are documented, waiting days to start drying and signing documents without reading them. Another is repairing the finish before the structure is dry, which can lead to mold and a second claim. Keep your timeline clear and your records organized.

Questions we hear in Deland

What is the first thing to do for a water damage claim?

Stop the water if it is safe, photograph the source and damage, and call your carrier. Start drying promptly and keep receipts.

Should I wait for the adjuster before drying?

Generally no. Policies commonly expect you to prevent further damage, so begin mitigation and document it.

Do restoration crews work with insurance companies in Deland?

Crews document damage and share records with your adjuster. Your policy decides what is paid.

Does Volusia County require permits for water damage repairs?

It can, depending on the work and location. Call Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591 before repairs.
