How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Deland, FL Home

A hidden leak shows up as a bill, a smell or a warm spot long before it shows up as a puddle. Here are checks you can do yourself.

By the Deland Water Damage Pros team

A hidden water leak is one that runs behind a wall, under a slab or above a ceiling where you cannot see it. The signs are often subtle, such as a higher water bill, a musty smell or a floor that feels warm. Most of the checks below take minutes and cost nothing.

Finding the leak early matters because water damage grows quietly. A leak that runs for weeks can soak insulation, rot framing and start mold.

Start with the water meter

Turn off every tap, appliance and irrigation zone, then look at the water meter. Most meters have a small leak indicator, often a triangle or a wheel, that spins when water is moving. If it moves with everything off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the house.

Take a photo of the meter reading, wait an hour with no water use and check again. A changed reading confirms the leak, and the amount helps a plumber narrow the source.

Compare your bills

Look at your water bills for the past several months. A steady climb with no change in habits points to a leak. City of Deland Utilities can tell you if the usage pattern looks unusual, and it can help rule out a problem on the street side of the meter.

Slab leaks and warm spots

Most Deland homes are slab-on-grade, which means pipes run through or under concrete. A leaking hot line can warm a patch of floor, and a leaking cold line can leave a damp spot under carpet or a cracked tile. If you notice either, call a plumber for leak detection before the floor is opened.

Our slab leak page explains how these leaks are handled after they are found.

Use your nose and eyes

  • A musty smell that comes and goes.
  • Paint bubbling or peeling on a wall or ceiling.
  • Baseboards that swell or pull away.
  • Cracked or lifted floor tiles.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.

A moisture meter and a thermal camera find wet spots without cutting walls, and they are what crews use to map a leak. If you suspect one, the wall and drywall repair page and the ceiling repair page explain what happens after it is found.

Deland details that help you search

City of Deland Utilities at 120 South Florida Avenue provides water, wastewater and reclaimed water. Customer service is at 386-626-7051, and the department has an emergency contact for broken mains and sewer backups.

Irrigation systems are a frequent hidden leak in Florida yards. If your yard has a soggy patch while the rest is dry, or your meter spins with the house off, shut off the irrigation zones one at a time and recheck.

Detection tools plumbers and crews use

Plumbers use acoustic listening devices, pressure tests and sometimes tracer gas to pinpoint a leak in a pipe you cannot see. Restoration crews use moisture meters and thermal cameras to map how far water has spread. The two sets of tools answer different questions, so you may need both.

A thermal camera does not see through walls. It shows temperature differences on the surface, and a wet area is often cooler than dry material around it. A crew confirms with a meter before anyone cuts drywall.

What to do when you find it

Shut off the water at the fixture or the main, photograph the damage and call a plumber. If water has soaked drywall, flooring or insulation, call a restoration crew so drying starts before mold does. Keep the failed part for your adjuster.

Neighbors in Orange City have their own utility, so call Orange City Utilities about usage questions there.

When the leak is outdoors

A leak between the meter and the house sits under yard soil and may show as a green, soggy strip or a sinking patch of lawn. A plumber can locate it with equipment and repair it, and your water utility can explain the usage record. Mark the spot with a flag so the repair crew can find it.

Insurance and slow leaks

Policies often treat slow leaks differently from sudden failures, and some exclude long-term seepage. Reading your policy now helps you understand what to expect. Florida Statute 627.70132 sets a one year notice window for property claims.

Questions we hear in Deland

How do I know if I have a hidden water leak?

Check your water meter with everything off. If it moves, water is escaping. Rising bills, musty smells and warm floor spots are other signs.

Can I find a slab leak myself?

You can spot the signs, but locating the exact spot needs a plumber with detection equipment.

Who do I call about a leak before the meter?

Call City of Deland Utilities if the leak is on the street side of the meter, and a plumber for leaks inside your property.

Do irrigation leaks matter inside the house?

They can if water runs toward the foundation. Soggy patches and a moving meter are the clues.
