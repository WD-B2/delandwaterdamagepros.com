Wall and drywall water damage repair is the drying and rebuilding of walls after water has soaked drywall, insulation and framing. Drywall acts like a sponge, pulling water up from the base and spreading it behind paint. If a wall has bubbles, stains or a musty smell, call and describe it, since the hidden side is usually worse than the visible side.

Water damage repair is what homeowners often search for once they see stains. The right order is dry first, repair second, because painting over wet drywall seals moisture in.

How water damages walls

Water rises up drywall by wicking, often reaching a foot or more above the visible line. Insulation in the wall cavity holds water against the back of the drywall, and paper facing gives mold an easy place to grow. In slab homes, the bottom plate of the wall sits on concrete that stays damp for days.

Common sources include supply lines, shower pans, roof leaks and AC drains. A stain on the wall of one room can start in the attic or bathroom above it.

Signs of wall water damage

Bubbling or peeling paint

Water pushing paint off the surface.

Soft or crumbly drywall

Gypsum core has weakened.

Stains that grow

Active moisture from a leak.

Musty odor

Mold or bacteria in the cavity.

What to do in the first hours

Find and stop the source, then move furniture away from the wall and lift curtains. Remove baseboards only if you can do so without damaging the wall, because open air at the base helps drying. Keep the room cool, since cooler air holds less moisture.

Mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and date it. If the mark grows after a day, water is still moving and the source has not been stopped.

Hidden wall moisture in older and newer homes

Newer Deland homes often have drywall over wood or metal framing with fiberglass insulation, which holds water against the back of the board. Older homes in the West Deland Residential District may have plaster over lath, which dries differently and can fail in sheets when saturated.

Stucco exteriors can hide leaks at windows and rooflines, and water may show up as a stain several feet from the real opening. A moisture meter and thermal camera trace the path so the repair addresses the source.

How crews dry and repair walls

Crews check moisture with meters and thermal imaging, remove baseboards and drill small holes to dry cavities with directed airflow. If drywall is soft or the insulation is wet, they cut out the wet section, usually at a straight line 2 feet above the floor. LGR dehumidifiers and air movers dry the framing before any closing begins.

Repair follows drying. Drywall is hung, taped, textured and painted to match. For ceilings, see our ceiling repair page.

Insurance and wall damage

Sudden leaks are commonly covered for resulting damage, and slow leaks can be excluded. Report within one year under Florida Statute 627.70132. Keep photos of the wall before opening it.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can wet drywall be saved?

Sometimes. If the water was clean and the wall was dried quickly, drywall can stay. Soft or stained drywall is usually cut out.

Why cut drywall 2 feet up?

Water wicks upward, and a straight cut above the wet line lets crews dry insulation and framing.

Can I paint over a water stain?

Only after the wall is dry and the source is fixed. Use a stain-blocking primer.

How long before mold grows in wet drywall?

Mold can begin in a day or two in warm, humid conditions.
