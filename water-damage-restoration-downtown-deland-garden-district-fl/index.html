Downtown DeLand is a National Register historic district, roughly bounded by Florida and Rich Avenues, Woodland Boulevard and Howry Avenue. The City of DeLand also lists the Garden Historic District next to it, a mixed-use area of older buildings. If water is in a shop, restaurant or apartment here, call the line and give the street address.

Why downtown buildings are handled differently

The city describes the downtown buildings as masonry, and MainStreet DeLand notes that a fire in September 1886 led the town to require brick or stone in the commercial district. Brick and plaster can hold moisture and dry slowly, so drying checks matter more than they do in a modern wood-frame room. Ask any crew how it will confirm that walls are dry before they are closed up.

Many downtown buildings share walls or floors with a neighbor. Water that starts in one unit can reach another, so tell the line if the leak may come from above, below or next door. Landlords and tenants should agree early on who contacts the property insurer.

Safe first steps in a commercial building

Keep customers and staff out of any area with standing water or hanging ceiling material.

Do not use electrical equipment in or near water, and call an electrician if the panel or outlets are wet.

Close food areas affected by sewage or dirty water and ask the agency that inspects your business what is required before reopening.

Records for the insurer and the landlord

Photograph the damage, the source and the stock or equipment affected. List what was lost with approximate values, and keep invoices and the moisture readings from drying visits. If you are a tenant, ask the landlord for the building policy contact, and ask your own insurer what a loss of income claim would need.

Whether a policy pays depends on its wording and the cause of the loss, so ask your insurer rather than assuming coverage. Keeping records in one place makes every conversation easier.

Flood maps and storms

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for this page. Mapped areas rarely follow district boundaries, so check the exact building on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center. Hurricane Ian brought about 13 inches of rain to DeLand in September 2022 according to a county briefing reported by the Daytona Beach News-Journal, but we have no record of how it affected individual downtown blocks.

Questions we hear in Downtown DeLand and Garden District

Does this page cover the Garden District?

Yes. The Garden Historic District is next to downtown and has a similar mix of older buildings, shops and homes, so the same steps apply. Give the line your street address.

Who calls the insurer when a tenant space floods?

Usually the tenant contacts their own insurer, and the landlord contacts the building insurer. Agree on this early because both may need photographs and drying records.

Can a restaurant reopen after a drain backup?

That depends on the cause, the cleanup and what the inspecting agency requires. Contact that agency before reopening, and keep cleanup records to show what was done.

How long does drying take in brick buildings?

There is no single number. Brick and plaster can take longer than drywall to reach a normal moisture range, so a crew should measure and repeat readings rather than work to a fixed schedule. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference for that kind of monitoring.
