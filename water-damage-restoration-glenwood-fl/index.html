Water problems we see around Glenwood

Heavy summer rain is the first cause. Many Glenwood lots are large and flat, and runoff has few places to go, so water can pool against a slab or run under a door. Walk your property after a storm and note where water stands, because that tells you where to extend a downspout or add fill.

Plumbing is the second. Homes on larger lots often have long supply runs from the street or a well, and a line that leaks under a slab or a yard can run for weeks unnoticed. A water bill that jumps, or a pressure drop in one fixture, is the clue to watch for.

Roof leaks are third. Older roofs on rural homes and outbuildings lose shingles to wind, and rain follows the gap into attic insulation and ceilings. Our ceiling water damage page explains how that repair is planned.

Emergency water removal in Glenwood

Same-day response is offered when available for active water, and nights and weekends follow the same process.

If water is still spreading in Glenwood

Do these in order while you wait

  • Shut off the main water valve, or the well pump breaker if your home has a private well.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms only from a dry breaker you can reach safely.
  • Lift small valuables and rugs off the floor and photograph the water line.
  • Keep children and pets out of water that came from a drain, a toilet or outdoors.

Land, homes and drainage around Glenwood Road

Glenwood sits along Glenwood Road, also known as County Road 4088, and borders the Lake Woodruff National Wildlife Refuge. Wetlands and low ground nearby mean the water table runs high after long rain, so crawl spaces and slab edges stay damp longer than they do in town. If your house is near the refuge edge, keep gutters clear and soil sloped away from the foundation.

Neighborhoods such as Glenwood Manor Farms, DeLeon Manor of Glenwood and Glenwood Plantation are a mix of rural lots and newer homes. When you call, tell the line whether your house is on a slab or raised, since that changes how we plan drying.

Permits, utilities and who to call

Glenwood is unincorporated, so building permits come from Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591. Ask before major repairs such as replacing walls or flooring, because the county tells you what needs a permit. Your water provider depends on your address, so check your bill or ask the provider before you assume who to call about a main-line problem.

If your home uses a septic system, tell the crew when you call. A backup from a septic tank is handled differently from a city sewer overflow, and our sewage backup page explains the safety steps.

History that still shows in the buildings

Glenwood grew from a citrus and lumber settlement that counted about 200 people in 1916, and a cypress lumber company arrived in 1922. Some older wood-frame homes and outbuildings from that era remain, and their rooflines and foundations tend to move water differently from newer slab homes, so mention the age of your house on the call.

Landmarks such as Mt. Olive A.M.E. Church and Citrus Grove Elementary on Hazen Road are reminders that the area is established, so older roofs and plumbing runs are worth checking each year. The Spring-to-Spring Trail passes nearby, a reminder of how much of the area is low, spring-fed ground.
