Lake Helen sits about five and a half miles southeast of Deland, and the line takes calls from there around the clock. The town is small, but its water problems follow the same seasons as the rest of West Volusia. This page walks through the year and then covers what is different here.

Water damage through the Lake Helen year

Summer is storm season. Afternoon thunderstorms drop heavy rain, and the hurricane season from June 1 to November 30 brings the larger events. Check roof flashing and window seals each May so a storm does not test them for you.

Fall brings the tail of hurricane season and the first cold fronts. Hurricane Ian in September 2022 and Hurricane Milton in October 2024 both reached Volusia County, so keep photos of your home as it was before a storm, which helps with a claim.

Winter is short and mild, but a hard freeze can crack exposed pipes. Wrap outdoor lines before a forecast freeze, and read our frozen pipe page for what to do when water appears after a thaw.

Spring is dry and is the best time for repairs. Clear gutters, test sump pumps if you have them and check AC drain lines before the heat returns.

Septic systems, city water and permits

Lake Helen has no city sewer, so most homes use septic systems, and septic permits come through the Volusia County Health Department. If a drain backs up, tell the line you are on septic, since a failed tank or drain field is a different problem from a city sewer main. Our sewage backup page explains the safety steps.

The city bills for water, and the city building department at 386-228-2531 uses Volusia County forms and fee schedules for permits. Call before major repairs to find out what you need.

Older buildings and what they teach

Lake Helen City Hall at 327 South Lakeview Drive was built in 1916 as an elementary school, and the city has had a project to replace its roof and windows. Old buildings like it show the typical failure points, which are roofs, window frames and flashing, so check yours the same way.

Homes from the early and mid twentieth century may have wood floors and plaster that react differently to water. Our hardwood floor page explains how crews save wood where they can.
