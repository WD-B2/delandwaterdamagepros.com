Water from inside or from outdoors

Water inside a home can come from a plumbing failure or from outdoors, and the safety rules differ. Clean water from a broken supply line, a water heater or an appliance is a different job from water that came from the ground, a lake, a drain or a sewer. The second kind is treated as contaminated.

If water is rising or the source is unclear

Stay out of water that may reach outlets, extension cords or appliances.

If the water came from outdoors, a drain or a sewer, keep children and pets away and do not use household fans or a vacuum on it.

If anyone in the home has limited mobility, tell the line so the plan accounts for it.

Extraction and drying

A crew removes standing water, then measures moisture in walls, trim and flooring. Structural drying uses dehumidifiers and air movers to bring those readings down, following practices set out in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. Materials that touched contaminated water are usually removed rather than dried.

If the roof or a window leaked in a storm, the repair of that opening is a separate step from drying. Ask the crew what they will do about temporary protection while you arrange the repair.

Rain, the lake and flood maps

Hurricane Ian brought about 13 inches of rain to DeLand in September 2022, according to a county briefing reported by the Daytona Beach News-Journal, and the National Weather Service issued flood warnings for the St. Johns River near DeLand. That was a regional event, and we have no evidence about how it affected homes around Lake Winnemissett in particular.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for this page. A lake view does not tell you your map zone, so search your own address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center, and ask your agent whether your policy treats flood differently from other water.

Records to keep

Photograph the source and the damage before anything is moved. Keep invoices, the moisture readings and a list of damaged belongings. Ask your insurer what it needs, since coverage depends on the policy wording and the cause.
