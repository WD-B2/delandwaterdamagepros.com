De Leon Springs is a community of about 2,600 people at the 2020 Census, roughly seven miles north of Deland, and the line takes calls from there around the clock. It is named for a spring, and water is part of daily life here. This page explains how that shapes damage and what to do when water comes in.

Where De Leon Springs water comes from

De Leon Springs State Park covers 625 acres and holds a spring that stays at about 72 degrees and moves around 20 million gallons a day. Spring-fed ground can stay wet after rain. Slab edges and crawl spaces nearby can stay damp for days, so look for efflorescence or damp baseboards after a storm.

Heavy rain, river rise and plumbing failure are the usual sources of water here. Each needs a different response, and the first step is the same, which is to stop what you can and call.

If water is still spreading in De Leon Springs

First steps while you wait

  • Shut the main water valve, or the well pump breaker if your home has a private well.
  • Switch off power to wet rooms only from a dry breaker and stay out of rooms with water at outlets.
  • Lift valuables, pull rugs off the floor and photograph the water line.
  • Keep children and pets out of water that came from outdoors or a drain.

Homes, packing houses and the old town

De Leon Springs was called Spring Garden in the late 1800s before it took its current name, and the state park has been a park since 1982. The Strawn Historic Citrus Packing House District still stands, and the Old Sugar Mill Pancake House is a landmark. Old wood buildings and large roofs need regular roof and gutter checks, since water that gets under old shingles rots decking slowly.

Homes in the area range from older frame houses to newer slab construction. Tell the crew which you have when you call, since drying a raised floor differs from drying a slab. The basement and lower-level page covers sunken garages and below-grade rooms.

Flood zones, permits and utilities

Volusia County states that every property is in a FEMA flood zone, so look up your address at msc.fema.gov. If your repairs are in a flood zone, the county may ask for substantial damage paperwork, so call Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591 before rebuilding. De Leon Springs is unincorporated and the county issues permits.

Water supply depends on your address, so check your bill or ask your provider before assuming who to call about a main-line issue. If you have a private well, tell the crew.
