Flood water carries silt and bacteria, and it soaks building materials fast. Crews pump, remove ruined materials and dry what can be saved.

Flood damage is an emergency, so call the 24/7 line as soon as water enters the house. Stay out of the water if it is deep or moving, keep power off at the panel only if you can reach it dry, and take photos of the water line on walls before cleanup. Do not wade through floodwater, because it carries sewage and debris.

Flood damage restoration is the cleanup of homes after outside water, such as river overflow, storm surge or heavy rain runoff, enters a building. Floodwater is treated as contaminated, which changes how materials are handled. Porous items such as carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that sat in it are usually removed.

When flood water is an emergency

Treat any water entering the house from outside as an emergency. Even a few inches soaks baseboards, door casings and the bottom of drywall, and it carries silt that dries into a film. Ask for same-day help, and tell the line whether the water is still rising or has started to recede.

Flood safety first

Floodwater and electricity are a deadly mix. Call the power utility or 911 if water has reached the panel or you smell gas, and do not enter a flooded room with live circuits.

Wear boots and gloves if you must enter. Contaminated water means cuts and skin contact need prompt cleaning.

Flood risk around Deland

Volusia County states that every property is in a FEMA flood zone and does not assign zones itself, so look up your address on the FEMA Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov. Knowing your zone helps you decide whether a flood policy is worth carrying before the next storm.

The St. Johns River near Deland set a record of 6.2 feet during Hurricane Ian in September 2022, passing the 1964 mark of 6.1 feet, according to local reporting of National Weather Service data. The river stayed in major flood stage for a long time, which is why homes near it dried slowly and needed monitoring for weeks.

Hurricane Milton in October 2024 brought flooding to Volusia County, and the county fairgrounds in Deland served as a shelter. If your street takes water in big storms, keep important papers off the floor and know your route before the rain begins.

What crews do after a flood

1

Pump

Submersible pumps remove deep water, often in stages so the structure is not stressed.

2

Remove

Wet carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that sat in floodwater are taken out.

3

Clean

Hard surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products.

4

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until moisture readings reach drying goals.

Crews use moisture meters and thermal imaging to find wet framing, and they keep a drying log so you have a record. Our water extraction page explains the pumping stage, and the structural drying page covers the equipment phase.

What drives flood cleanup cost

  1. Depth and duration of the water in the house.
  2. Square footage affected and the number of floors.
  3. Amount of material that must be removed.
  4. Drying equipment days and monitoring visits.
  5. Whether contaminated water requires extra disposal steps.

Flood insurance and documentation

Standard homeowners policies commonly exclude flood from rising water, which is why a separate flood policy matters. Check your policies and ask your agent before a storm, not after. Florida Statute 627.70132 sets a one year window for giving notice of a property claim, so report promptly.

Volusia County emergency permitting information mentions a substantial damage assessment and a FEMA elevation certificate for repairs in flood zones. Call Volusia County Building and Code Administration at 386-626-6591 before rebuilding to ask what applies to your property.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Deland?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, including during storms. Call when it is safe to do so and describe the water depth. Crews are scheduled based on conditions, and roads may limit access during severe weather.

Is floodwater always contaminated?

Treat it as contaminated. Outdoor flood water carries sewage, soil and chemicals, so porous materials that soaked in it are usually removed.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

Most standard policies exclude flood from rising water, and a separate flood policy is the usual path. Check your declarations page and ask your agent.

How do I find my flood zone?

Volusia County does not assign zones. Look up your address on the FEMA Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov, or ask the county building department.
