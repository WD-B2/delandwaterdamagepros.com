Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Deland, FL

Sewage is a health hazard. Crews extract contaminated water, remove porous materials and sanitize, so the home is safe again.

Sewage backup is an emergency, so stop using water, keep people and pets out of the affected area and call the 24/7 line right away. Do not flush toilets or run sinks, because that pushes more waste into the house. Wear boots and gloves if you must pass through, and wash well afterward.

Sewage backup cleanup is the safe removal of contaminated water and the sanitizing of everything it touched. Sewage is classed as Category 3 water, which means it can carry bacteria and viruses. Porous materials such as carpet, pad and drywall that absorbed it are usually removed.

Sewage backup: what to do first

Stop using drains and toilets, and move to another bathroom if one is unaffected. Call the line and describe where the sewage is coming from, such as a floor drain, a tub or a toilet. If the backup is from the city line, call City of Deland Utilities at 386-626-7051, because a main blockage may be the utility's responsibility.

Health and electrical safety

Keep children, older adults and pets out of sewage water. Do not eat or drink in the area.

Do not use household vacuums on sewage, and stay away from outlets and appliances in wet rooms until power is off at a dry breaker.

Why is water coming up from my floor drain or toilet?

Gurgling drains, slow flushing and a sewer smell often come before a backup. Water rising in a tub or floor drain when the washer runs points to a blockage in the main line. If every drain in the house is slow, the problem is likely in the main, and a plumber needs to clear it.

A blockage can be roots in an older clay or cast iron line, grease, or items flushed that should not be. Homes on septic systems, common in unincorporated areas around Deland, can back up when the tank is full or the drain field fails.

How crews clean a sewage backup

1

Contain

The affected area is isolated so contamination does not spread through the house.

2

Extract

Contaminated water is pumped out with extraction units.

3

Remove and clean

Porous materials are removed and hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

4

Dry

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged.

Crews wear protective gear and use antimicrobial products suited to sewage. Moisture meters and thermal imaging check that nothing wet stays behind walls. Odor control follows once surfaces are clean.

What homeowners should not do after a backup

Do not try to dry sewage-soaked carpet with fans, because fans spread contamination through the air and into other rooms. Do not mix bleach with other cleaners, since some combinations release toxic gas. Do not move wet drywall or insulation through clean areas of the house.

Do keep photos and notes of what the sewage touched, since an adjuster will ask. Keep children and pets out until a crew confirms the area is clean and dry.

Insurance and sewage backups

Sewage backup coverage often requires an endorsement on a homeowners policy, so check yours before you need it. Report within one year under Florida Statute 627.70132, and keep photos and a record of any plumber visit.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Do you handle sewage backup emergencies at night or on weekends in Deland?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7. Stay out of the area and call. A crew is arranged to extract and sanitize.

Why is sewage coming up my floor drain?

A blockage in the main line is the usual cause. A plumber clears the line, and we clean up what came out.

Is it safe to clean up sewage myself?

It is not recommended. Sewage carries pathogens, and porous materials must be removed.

Who is responsible, me or the city?

If the blockage is in the street main, the utility may be responsible. If it is in your lateral, you are. A plumber can tell where it is.
