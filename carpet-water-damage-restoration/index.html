Carpet water damage restoration is the extraction, drying and cleaning of carpet and pad after water gets into them. Carpet can often be saved if the water was clean and extraction started quickly, while the pad beneath is frequently replaced. If your carpet squishes underfoot or smells musty, call and describe it.

Keep foot traffic off wet carpet, lift furniture onto foil or blocks and open interior doors for airflow. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet.

How water gets into carpet

Water enters from above with a leak, from below through a slab crack or from the sides through baseboards. Central Florida homes often have carpet over slab, where moisture rises from the concrete and the pad acts as a sponge. If a bedroom carpet feels cool and damp near an exterior wall, check for a slab leak or a rain path.

Water category matters. Clean water from a supply line can be dried, while sewage or flood water means the carpet and pad usually come out. Our sewage backup cleanup page explains that side.

Signs of carpet water damage

Squishy or cold spots

Pad is holding water.

Musty smell

Mold or bacteria in the pad.

Stains or browning

Tannins and dirt wick up from the pad.

Loose seams

Backing detaches when saturated.

What to do in the first hours

Stop the source, then remove small items and furniture from the wet area. Blot standing water with towels and keep fans or the AC running for airflow, though fans alone will not dry carpet over a wet pad. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs so stain does not bleed into the fibers.

Photograph the room and the water source, and call your carrier if you plan to file. A crew can usually tell from a quick description whether the pad needs to come out, so call early.

Carpet types and how they dry

Nylon and polyester carpet tolerate water better than wool or natural fibers, which can brown and shrink. Berber loops can snag when lifted, so lifting is done carefully. Backing type matters too, since latex backing can delaminate when it stays soaked.

Older carpet that is already near the end of its life may not justify a restoration. A crew will tell you honestly whether drying or replacing makes more sense for your floor.

How crews dry carpet

Crews extract water with weighted tools, check moisture with meters and thermal imaging and decide whether the pad can stay. Carpet is often lifted at a corner, propped on a rack and dried with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers. Antimicrobial treatment is used where needed.

The pad is rarely worth saving after a soak because it holds water and bacteria. Replacing it costs far less than redoing a carpet that smells.

What affects carpet restoration price

  1. Square footage wet.

Insurance and carpet damage

Sudden leaks are often covered for resulting damage, and policies commonly depreciate carpet by age. Report within one year under Florida Statute 627.70132, and keep photos and a sample of the carpet.

The industry standard behind the work

Professional water damage restoration follows a published industry document, the ANSI/IICRC S500. It sets out how a loss is assessed, how affected materials are classified and how drying or cleanup is documented.

Ask any company how it applies that standard on your job. A clear answer should cover readings taken, equipment placed and the records you will receive. This page describes the approach, and it is not a claim about any individual certificate.

Questions we hear in Deland

Can wet carpet be saved?

Often yes if the water was clean and extraction began quickly. The pad is usually replaced.

How long can carpet stay wet before mold starts?

Mold can begin in a day or two in warm, humid conditions. Prompt drying prevents it.

Is it safe to walk on wet carpet?

Avoid it if the water might be contaminated or near electrical devices.

Should I pull up carpet myself?

A crew can lift it safely and protect the tack strips. DIY pulling can tear backing.
